# Response to Referee Report (SIFIN)

All major comments (M1–M7), all minor comments (1–15), and all four questions
have been addressed. The estimator itself was changed (M3), so all empirical
numbers were regenerated by re-running `experiment/experiment_rmt.py`; the
implementation changes live in `src/fast_minimum_variance/` and the paper
changes in `paper_rmt/`.

## Major comments

**M1 — dual-feasibility gradient.** The referee was right that the printed
algorithm used the sample-covariance gradient while the implementation used the
correct objective gradient $2\,T_0^{\mathrm{RMT}}w - \rho\hat\mu$. The paper
(Section 2, Algorithm 1 line 12, and the surrounding discussion) now states the
correct gradient, evaluated through the low-rank form at $O(nk)$; the return
matrix has been removed from Algorithm 1's inputs, and the text now states —
correctly — that $X$ never enters the solve. The implementation was further
tightened so that the $O(nT)$ data matvec is skipped at $\alpha=1$
(`_compute_gradient`). As requested, the paper now reports direct optimality
cross-checks: $\|w_{\mathrm{WB}} - w_{\mathrm{Clarabel}}\|_\infty \le 2.5\times10^{-4}$,
with the Woodbury solution attaining a strictly lower objective (relative
difference $3.3\times10^{-5}$), i.e. the discrepancy is interior-point
truncation (Section 7.1, "Correctness").

**M2 — crossover constant.** Replaced throughout. Proposition 4.2 now derives
the exact crossover: with $x = k/n_a$, the unit-constant cubic factors as
$(x+1)^2(x-1)$ and the FLOP-constant cubic as $(x-1)(x^2+4x+1)$, so the
threshold is $k < n_a$ in both cases. The $1.618$ constant, the "lower-order
terms" approximation, and the "52%" sentence are gone. Remark 4.3 reports the
measured ratios ($x \approx 0.2$–$0.7$ in our experiments), notes the
$\approx 3\times$ FLOP prediction at $x \approx 0.5$ versus the $5\times$
measured warm advantage, and labels the cache explanation as consistent-with
rather than established.

**M3 — trace preservation.** The estimator was changed to the standard
trace-preserving CRE: $\bar\lambda$ is now the bulk average
$(\mathrm{tr}\hat\Sigma - \sum_{j\le k}\lambda_j)/(n-k)$, in both the library
(`shrinkage/util.py`) and the experiments. Proposition 3.1 now states
$\mathrm{tr}(T_0) = \mathrm{tr}(\hat\Sigma)$, the "bulk mean eigenvalue"
terminology is fixed ($\hat\sigma^2 = \mathrm{tr}/n$ is the grand mean and sets
the MP edge; $\bar\lambda$ is the bulk mean), and the attribution now reads
"exactly the CRE estimator of Laloux et al. and Bun–Bouchaud–Potters", which
is now true. All numbers were regenerated: $\kappa(T_0) \approx 343$ (was 146),
$k$-sensitivity $\le 33$ bp (was 12 bp), etc.

**M4 — statistical overclaim and missing positioning.** The "statistically
correct operation" claim is deleted. Remark 2.1 now gives the honest, algebraic
reason for $\alpha = 1$. A new Remark 3.2 positions CRE against the oracle RIE
(Ledoit–Péché overlaps) and nonlinear shrinkage (Ledoit–Wolf 2012, 2020; Bun et
al. 2017), states openly that these dominate clipping in Frobenius loss but are
not Woodbury-compatible, and notes the BBP edge bias. A new out-of-sample
section (7.4) reports a rolling backtest: RMT-CRE 9.91% OOS vol vs 9.74/9.75%
for linear shrinkage and 14.54% for equal weight, with the explicit statement
that no superiority is claimed — only the absence of a measurable penalty.

**M5 — novelty positioning.** The introduction now cites Perold (1984) for
Woodbury/factor structure in portfolio QPs, frames the contribution as the
combination (RMT-determined structure + rSVD preprocessing + end-to-end
accounting), and softens "all existing implementations" to public-domain
implementations. A new Remark 4.1 relates the warm-started sweep to the
critical line algorithm (Markowitz 1956; Niedermayer & Niedermayer 2010) and
explains when each is appropriate.

**M6 — Proposition 5.1.** Restated. The proposition now applies Halko et al.
Theorem 10.6 to $\hat\Sigma$ itself (the sketch $Y = T\hat\Sigma\Omega$ is a
range finder for $\hat\Sigma$), bounds $\|(I-P)\hat\Sigma\|_2$ in expectation,
and converts to eigenvalue error via Weyl's inequality with the
$\|\hat\Sigma - P\hat\Sigma P\| \le 2\|(I-P)\hat\Sigma\|$ symmetrisation — all
stated and proved. The false trace-lifted Frobenius bound is gone. The
0.14 bp heuristic chain is replaced by a direct measurement: solving with rSVD
vs dense eigenpairs moves no weight by more than 2.3 bp (now an experiment
output, quoted in Sections 5 and 8).

**M7 — algorithm/prose/proof/code consistency.** Algorithm 1 now performs
single-asset exchanges in both the primal and dual steps, matching the prose
and the termination proof (which requires single exchanges); the
implementation's bulk-drop acceleration and safeguards are disclosed in a new
Remark 2.2, with the observation that they never triggered and the converged
weights match interior-point solutions. The median justification is now exact
(with the corrected gradient, all active gradient entries equal $2\theta$ at
the subproblem optimum). A new Remark 2.3 addresses the tolerance scale.

## Beyond the report

Three further additions strengthen the revision past the report's asks:

- **Second out-of-sample universe.** The backtest now covers the FTSE 100
  ($n/L \approx 0.17$, 57 rebalances) alongside the S&P 500
  ($n/L \approx 0.98$), with a disclosed data filter excluding dead/stale
  assets (a zero-variance delisted name would otherwise absorb the entire
  minimum-variance portfolio). The CRE portfolio is within 0.1 volatility
  points of linear shrinkage on the S&P 500 and lowest outright on the FTSE.
- **Incremental eigenpair updates implemented** (formerly future work):
  a rank-two update of the tracked basis costs 0.21 ms/day against 4.8 ms for
  a fresh sketch (23×), with median portfolio drift of 6.7 bp over 100 days
  without any re-sketch.
- **Critical line algorithm on the Woodbury kernel** (formerly a remark):
  an exact parametric sweep traces all 144 frontier breakpoints in 9 ms — the
  cost of the 50-point grid — and attains an objective at least as low as the
  grid solver's at every grid point.
